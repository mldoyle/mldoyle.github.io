Thesis¶

This research is motivated by the order-driven view of markets, in which trading activity and available liquidity play a central role in price formation. Both informed and uninformed trades can affect prices. Institutional investors often divide large orders into smaller trades executed over time, allowing buying or selling pressure to persist across successive observations. Some of the resulting price impact may decay after execution, while a longer-lasting component remains. Bouchaud, The Inelastic Market Hypothesis: A Microstructural Interpretation.

Institutional mandates and allocation targets limit how readily investors adjust their holdings in response to price changes. When other investors have limited flexibility to absorb a demand shock, prices may need to move considerably to accommodate it. Gabaix and Koijen. Funds with mechanical rebalancing rules, including ETF products, can also generate partly predictable trading pressure. Brøgger.

Our hypothesis is that classified order-flow history can help identify latent demand and predict subsequent relative returns. Repeated institutional buying or selling may indicate a larger position being accumulated or unwound. The relationship between order flow and the accompanying price response may help distinguish continuing pressure from activity whose impact has already been absorbed. Whether these patterns contain useful information about future returns remains an empirical question.

We test this hypothesis using measures of institutional and retail flow, their persistence, and their relationship with prices. The objective is to determine whether these measures predict returns after accounting for common risk factors, and whether that predictive information can support a portfolio after estimated trading costs.

Risk Model¶

The risk model is a statistical model which uses principal component analysis (PCA) to estimate dominant covariance directions. Principal component analysis takes the first $ k $ eigenvectors of the covariance matrix using centered standardized 252 day trailing mean returns $ z = \frac{x - \hat{\mu}}{\hat{\sigma}} $ The risk model is refitted each day at time t using the previous days The top $k$ principal componenets (factor loadings) are used to estimate factor returns:

The risk model uses principal component analysis (PCA) to identify the dominant patterns of co-movement across stock returns. Each stock’s daily returns are centered by subtracting their trailing mean and standardized by dividing by their trailing standard deviation:

$ z=\frac{x-\hat{\mu}}{\hat{\sigma}} $

PCA identifies the eigenvectors corresponding to the $ k$ largest eigenvalues of the covariance matrix of these standardized returns. These directions capture the greatest variation in the return panel. We estimate the model using a rolling window of up to 252 trading sessions, require at least 126 valid observations per stock, and retain the first 10 componenets, (factor loadings) which are used to estimate factor returns:

$ R_t = B_t f_t + \varepsilon_t $ and these are subtracted from the raw returns to product the learning target $ \hat{R} = \varepsilon_t $

Label definition and timing¶

For a decision made after session (t), the raw target is the return from the next session’s open to the open (H) sessions later: $ \frac{O_{i,t+1+H}}{O_{i,t+1}}-1 $

We tested 1, 10 and 20 day holding periods. The current configuration uses (H=10). Features use the 16:01 New York cutoff on session (t), before the label window begins. The model learns the component of this forward return remaining after the fitted common-factor contribution is removed.

Signal¶

A cross-gradient boosted tree (xgb) model is used to fit the engineered features to residual returns: $\varepsilon_t = R_t - B_t f_t$ The model is initally trained on a 3 year period (756 sessions) with 126 validation and test holdouts, including a $ h $ day purge period to avoid leakage from holding period forward returns, followed by walk forward expanding folds.

Features are selected using training data, and validation determines the tree count. The selected model is then refitted on the eligible pre-test history, including the earlier validation observations, before producing predictions for the next test block.

Samples near the training–validation and validation–test boundaries are removed to prevent their forward labels from overlapping the following block. The final refit also excludes observations whose labels extend into the test period. The exclusion uses the label’s endpoint offset, H+1. There is no separate post-test embargo. Earlier test observations may enter later training folds once their labels have matured.

The models signal is monotonically predictive of forward residual returns, stable and positive across sub periods with a high degree of confidence, all statistically significant at the 5% threshold. The models signal improves over time suggesting benefits from an expanded training dataset.

Portfolio¶

The strategy constructs a dollar-neutral, long-short portfolio. It buys the top 10% of stocks ranked by the signal and shorts the bottom 10%.

Positions are sized using inverse residual volatility, so lower-risk stocks receive larger weights:

$$ |w_{i,t}^{\mathrm{sleeve}}| \propto \frac{1}{H\sigma_{i,t}} $$

A new $1/H$-sized sleeve is added each day and held for $H$ trading sessions. The long and short sides are normalized to equal notional exposure.

The active sleeves are combined into the alpha portfolio:

$$ a_t = \sum_{j=1}^{H} w_t^{(j)} $$

A separate hedge sleeve is recalculated daily to remove exposure to the PCA risk factors. If $B_t$ contains the stocks’ factor loadings, the alpha portfolio’s factor exposure is $B_t^\top a_t$. The hedge is selected so that:

$$ B_t^\top h_t = -B_t^\top a_t $$

The combined portfolio is therefore approximately factor-neutral:

$$ B_t^\top(a_t + h_t) = 0 $$

Among feasible hedges, the implementation favors lower residual risk and lower turnover.

We also tested more complex approaches, including mean-variance optimization. However, estimating a full covariance matrix across roughly 500 stocks introduced many noisy and unstable covariance directions, which is consistent with portfolio optimization research. Inverse-volatility sizing was more robust.

side note:

  • an unconstrained hedge, not forced to be dollar neutral consistently prefered solutions that were net long. indicating that the portfolio is negatively exposed to market beta? I decided that limiting our overall exposure to be net neutral was a more reasonable approach to risk management. since the backtest does not have a major market drawdown outside of early 2025 and during periods of strong market performance it is natural that being net long would yeild stronger results.
  • allowing a variable hedge size, instead of rescaling the entire book to fit the gross leverage target can improve gross, by maintaining a targetted level of alpha exposure. but withhout a hard gross cap this could potentially lead to higher leverage during periods of higher risk. however rescaling the full book has the effect of percentage of the book dedicated to the hedge during periods of high risk exposure, and potentially lower volatility.

Execution and Backtest¶

Decisions use session-(t) information, with execution modeled at the following (t+1) session’s open. Our backtest assumes executing using Market on Open (MOO) orders. Live execution requires the vendor data to arrive and processing to finish before order submission. Vendor data is finalized around 2:00AM before opening session t+1.

Based on a sample 2.5 year backtest we see strong performance of between 1 and 2 Sharpe with 5 bps of slippage, minimal drawdowns. Extech's whitepapers show further evidence that the strategy is stable over multiple market regeimes inlcuding in 2008 and 2020, though larger scale testing of this particular strategy would be a logical next step.

The cost assumption is a general assumption of costs in liquid equities. Borrow fees, financing, and size-dependent market impact are not separately estimated. MOO orders technically guarentee fills at the printed opening price, but are generally illiquid and does not guarentee matching orders for relatively less liquid names. The existing capacity calculation (below) varies assumed auction volume and participation using historical daily dollar volume. It is a sensitivity estimate. Auction data, initial portfolio construction, and the minimum-trade filter should be incorporated before making a firm capacity claim.

The relative underperfromance in the later folds might be associated with sector rotation during June-July 2026 as well as relatively higher historical volumes and volatilities.

Further evaluation should compare performance across subperiods and under various cost assumptions. Using historical bid/ask would allow us to produce a more robust estimate of the spread and the costs of execution.
